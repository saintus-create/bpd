# Anxiety and panic

> Sample document for the bpd demo. General information only — not medical
> advice.

## What anxiety is

Anxiety is the body's response to perceived threat. It is normal and useful in
moderate amounts — it sharpens attention before a deadline or a difficult
conversation. It becomes a problem when it is persistent, out of proportion,
or starts to interfere with daily life.

Anxiety disorders are among the most common mental health conditions, and they
are treatable.

## Common signs

Anxiety shows up in the body and in thinking:

- racing or repetitive thoughts, difficulty concentrating
- restlessness, irritability, feeling on edge
- a tight chest, fast heartbeat, sweating, nausea
- sleep problems, or waking early
- avoiding situations that trigger the feeling

Avoidance brings short-term relief and tends to make anxiety worse over time,
because the feared situation is never tested.

## Panic attacks

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear that peaks within minutes. It
can include a pounding heart, shortness of breath, dizziness, chest pain and a
sense of unreality. Panic attacks are frightening and feel dangerous, but they
are not medically dangerous in themselves.

Many people who have one panic attack go on to avoid the places where it
happened. That avoidance is what often needs treatment, rather than the attack
itself.

## What helps

Evidence-based approaches include:

- **Talking therapies**, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT),
  which is the most studied treatment for anxiety.
- **Medication**, for some people and some conditions, prescribed and
  monitored by a clinician.
- **Breathing and grounding techniques**, useful for riding out a spike.
- **Regular sleep, movement and reduced stimulants**, which lower baseline
  arousal.

## When to get help

See a professional if anxiety is persistent, if you are avoiding things you
want to do, if panic attacks recur, or if you are using alcohol or other
substances to cope.

If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself, see
`crisis-support.md`.
