# Depression and low mood

> Sample document for the bpd demo. General information only — not medical
> advice.

## Low mood and depression

Everyone has flat days. Depression is different: it is persistent, it affects
how a person functions, and it does not lift in response to good news or rest.

Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions and is among the
leading causes of disability worldwide. It is treatable, and most people who
get treatment improve.

## Common signs

- low mood or emptiness most of the day, most days
- loss of interest or pleasure in things that used to matter
- changes in sleep — too much or too little
- changes in appetite and weight
- fatigue, or feeling slowed down
- difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- guilt, worthlessness, or a sense of being a burden
- thoughts of death or self-harm

A diagnosis generally requires several of these to persist for two weeks or
more. Only a qualified professional can make it.

## What helps

- **Talking therapies** such as CBT, behavioural activation and
  interpersonal therapy.
- **Medication**, usually an antidepressant, when it is appropriate — it is
  typically prescribed and reviewed by a clinician, and takes some weeks to
  take effect.
- **Structured routine** — sleep, daylight, movement and contact with other
  people all have measurable effects on mood.
- **Combination treatment**, which is often more effective than either alone
  for moderate to severe depression.

## Supporting someone else

Listen without rushing to fix it. Take talk of self-harm seriously. Help with
practical things. Encourage professional contact, and offer to go along if
that helps.

## When to get help

Seek help promptly if low mood lasts more than two weeks, if you are not
functioning, or if you have thoughts of harming yourself. If you are in
immediate danger, see `crisis-support.md`.
