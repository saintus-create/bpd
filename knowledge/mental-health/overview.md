# Mental health: an overview

> Sample document for the bpd demo. General information only — not medical
> advice.

## What mental health means

Mental health describes how a person thinks, feels and copes with ordinary
life. It sits on a spectrum: everyone has good and bad periods, and most
people will struggle at some point without that amounting to a diagnosis.

Mental health conditions are common. The World Health Organization estimates
that around one in eight people globally lives with a mental health condition,
and that depression and anxiety are among the leading causes of disability
worldwide.

## Common categories

Clinicians group conditions broadly rather than by single symptoms:

- **Anxiety disorders** — persistent, hard-to-control worry or fear.
- **Mood disorders** — depression and bipolar disorder.
- **Trauma-related conditions** — responses to overwhelming events.
- **Psychotic disorders** — changes in perception and thinking.
- **Substance use disorders** — harmful patterns of use.

These categories overlap, and the same person may experience more than one.

## What affects mental health

Mental health is shaped by biology, circumstances and experience together:
genetics, physical health, sleep, money and housing, work, relationships,
discrimination, and major life events such as bereavement.

That mix matters because it means mental health is not a matter of willpower.
"It's a chemical imbalance" and "just think positive" are both incomplete
explanations.

## When to seek help

Consider talking to a professional if low mood, anxiety or other symptoms:

- last more than a couple of weeks,
- are affecting work, study, sleep or relationships,
- feel out of proportion to what triggered them, or
- come with thoughts of harming yourself.

Reaching out early tends to make things easier, but help is worthwhile at any
point.

## Where to start

A general practitioner is usually the first port of call. From there, a person
may be referred to a psychologist, psychiatrist, counsellor or community
service. In a crisis, contact an emergency service or a helpline directly —
see `crisis-support.md`.
